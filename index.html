The clipboard you already use, on both.

Press Ctrl+C on your computer and the text is on your phone a second later, ready to paste. No app to open, no button to press.

Going the other way: Android doesn’t let apps read your clipboard in the background, so the phone side is one tap on a Quick Settings tile. Passwords from password managers are never sent.

Photos and files, any size.

Share from your gallery or any app on the phone and it lands in Downloads\Dropline on your PC. Going the other way, drop files onto the Dropline window.

Speed: it’s your Wi‑Fi, not the internet, so a 1 GB video takes about as long as copying it across your network. You see progress, can cancel, and get a check mark when it’s done.

Your phone, in a corner of your PC.

Share your phone’s screen and it floats above everything in a small window you can drag anywhere. Double‑click for fullscreen. Turn the phone sideways and the window turns too.

Also the other way: your PC’s screen on your phone, with pinch to zoom and picture‑in‑picture. There’s no sound, and fast video can stutter.

Notifications, without picking up the phone.

Messages, reminders and alerts appear above the taskbar, even with Dropline tucked in the tray. Close one on the PC and it’s gone from the phone too.

You choose: the sound, how long they stay, which corner, and which apps are allowed. You can also hide message text, which is handy when sharing your screen.

Lost the phone in the sofa again?

Click the bell next to your phone and it rings at full volume, even on silent, until you tap “Found it” or open the app. The phone can ring the PC too.

Battery: your phone’s battery shows next to its name on the PC, with a warning when it drops to 20%.

Set up in about a minute

Install on both

Run Dropline-Setup.exe on your PC and install the APK on your phone. Neither needs an account.

Scan the code

Click + on the PC to show a QR code, and scan it from the phone. Or pick the PC from the Nearby list and check the 6‑digit code matches.

That’s it

They find each other on your Wi‑Fi from then on, and both update themselves when a new version comes out.

Nothing goes through anyone else’s server.

Dropline has no servers. Your devices talk to each other directly, and nobody else can listen in.

  • Each device makes its own key. Connections are encrypted and locked to the devices you paired.
  • Everything saved is encrypted, with the key held by Windows or the Android Keystore.
  • No account, no analytics, no ads, no tracking. Nothing is ever uploaded.
  • Optional PIN lock, history that deletes itself, and a log of every pairing and connection attempt.

Questions

Windows says “Windows protected your PC”. Is it safe?

That warning appears for any program that isn’t signed with a paid certificate, which Dropline doesn’t have yet. Click More info, then Run anyway. The installer doesn’t ask for administrator access, and installs only into your own user folder.

Does it work away from home, over mobile data?

No. Both devices need to be on the same Wi‑Fi. That’s the price of having no servers: nothing ever leaves your own network.

Why does the phone ask for “Notification access”?

That’s how an Android app can see your notifications in order to show them on your PC. It’s only used for that, and only if you turn on “Send notifications to computer”. If Android greys it out as a “restricted setting”, open Dropline’s App info, tap ⋮ and choose “Allow restricted settings”.

Why can’t the phone send its clipboard automatically?

Since Android 10, only the app you’re looking at can read the clipboard, to protect you from snooping apps. So sending from the phone is one tap: the Quick Settings tile, the notification button, or the Clipboard button in the app.

Is it free? What’s the catch?

It’s free, and there isn’t one: no ads, no account, no data collection, no paid tier.

How do I uninstall it?

On the PC: Settings → Uninstall in Dropline, or Windows Settings → Installed apps. Either way you can choose to delete your data too. Files you received stay in your Downloads folder. On the phone: Settings → Uninstall, or hold the icon and uninstall as usual.
